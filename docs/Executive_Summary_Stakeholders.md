## Overview

EduPro's 2025 instructor performance evaluation reveals a platform experiencing significant quality stratification across its 60-instructor, 60-course portfolio. While top-performing instructors drive exceptional learner demand — attracting 3.72× more enrollments than the platform average — nearly half of all instructors fall in the lowest experience bracket, producing ratings that fall well below desirable thresholds. This brief summarises the three most critical findings and provides prioritised recommendations for institutional action.

## Finding 1: Teaching Experience Is a Strong Predictor of Instructor Quality — But Not Course Quality

> **The 6-year threshold is the single most actionable lever for platform quality improvement.**

Statistical analysis confirms that teaching experience is a **strong, significant predictor of instructor ratings** (Pearson r = 0.598, p < 0.001). Instructors with 0–5 years of experience average a rating of just **2.40 out of 5**, while those with 16+ years average **4.78 out of 5** — a 2.38-point gap.

Critically, however, experience does **not** improve course quality ratings (r = −0.013, p = 0.696). This means that **hiring experienced instructors alone will not improve learner satisfaction with course content**.

**Institutional Implication:** EduPro must invest in two parallel programmes:
- **Instructor mentorship**: Pair junior instructors (0–5 years) with senior mentors to accelerate rating improvement.
- **Curriculum development**: Commission independent course design reviews to improve content quality regardless of instructor experience level.

---

## Finding 2: Instructor Quality Does Not Transfer to Course Quality — A Structural Design Gap

> **High instructor ratings and high course ratings are independent outcomes that require independent investment.**

The Pearson correlation between TeacherRating and CourseRating across 887 instructor-course records is **r = −0.034** (statistically not significant, p = 0.317). This near-zero relationship means that the platform cannot rely on its best instructors to automatically produce its best-rated courses.

Evidence:
- **Machine Learning** instructors are the platform's highest-rated cohort (avg 4.23/5), yet their courses produce the **lowest average course rating** (2.66/5).
- **Marketing** courses, despite moderate instructor ratings, achieve the **highest average course rating** (3.72/5).

**Institutional Implication:** EduPro should establish a dedicated **Instructional Design Unit** to evaluate and improve course structure, content sequencing, assessment quality, and learner support independently from instructor hiring and retention decisions.

---

## Finding 3: Instructor Ratings Are the Dominant Demand Driver — Creating an Enrollment Equity Gap

> **High-rated instructors attract 3.72× more learners — concentrating platform value in a small elite cohort.**

Instructors rated 4.0 and above (approximately 16.7% of the instructor pool) attract an average of **620 enrollments each**, compared to the platform average of **166.67** and low-rated instructor average of just **64.9**. This concentration creates a structural inequality:

- Learners in courses taught by lower-rated instructors receive a lower-quality learning experience AND fewer peer interactions due to lower enrollment.
- Platform revenue is disproportionately concentrated among a small number of high-performing instructors.
- Low-rated instructors, if retained without improvement support, undermine platform credibility.

**Institutional Implication:** A **two-pronged enrollment equity strategy** is required:
1. Algorithmically promote high-rated instructors to maximise learner outcomes and platform revenue.
2. Implement a formal **performance improvement programme** with measurable milestones for instructors below 3.0 — with transparent criteria for retention decisions.

## Strategic Recommendations (Prioritised)

### Priority 1 — Immediate (0–3 months)
1. **Launch a Mentorship Programme** pairing all 28 instructors in the 0–5 year experience band with senior instructors. Target: raise group average from 2.40 to ≥ 3.0 within 12 months.
2. **Performance Review** for all 10 instructors rated below 2.0, with 90-day improvement plans and clear retention criteria.

### Priority 2 — Short-Term (3–6 months)
3. **Curriculum Redesign Initiative** for Machine Learning, Business, and Web Development courses — targeting categories with ratings below 2.85.
4. **Advanced Course Audit**: All 21 Advanced-level courses average 2.81/5. A learner feedback survey and content scaffold review should be commissioned immediately.
5. **Instructional Design Unit Establishment**: Hire or contract instructional designers to separate curriculum quality from instructor quality in the evaluation framework.

### Priority 3 — Medium-Term (6–12 months)
6. **Update Recommendation Algorithm** to weight TeacherRating more heavily in course surfacing — aligning platform incentives with instructor quality.
7. **Dual KPI Dashboard**: Deploy the EduPro Analytics Suite (Streamlit dashboard) as the platform's live monitoring tool for quarterly stakeholder reporting.
8. **Recruit in Design and Business**: These two domains have the fewest instructors (4 and 2 respectively) with the lowest ratings — targeted hiring is more efficient than development alone.

## Conclusion

EduPro stands at an inflection point. The data confirms that the platform possesses exceptional instructors capable of delivering transformative learner outcomes — but also a substantial cohort of underperforming instructors and poorly-designed courses that are suppressing platform credibility and learner engagement. The path forward requires **simultaneous, parallel investment** in instructor development and curriculum quality, guided by a clear dual-KPI framework and supported by the real-time analytics infrastructure now available through the EduPro Analytics Suite.
